## 1. Summary

This note records a user-submitted account of three Google Maps listings that reportedly refer to residences associated with Prabowo Subianto but display markedly different names and categories:

| Reported listing name | Reported imagery/date | Reported category | Reported rating |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Das Haus von Prabowo Subianto | February 2026 | Historical landmark | 4.8 (49 reviews) |
| Rumah juang PRABOWO SUBIANTO | January 2024 | Housing complex | 3.9 (64 reviews) |
| Rumah Prabowo Subianto, cabang Kertanegara | April 2022 | Homestay | 3.6 (58 reviews) |

**Important:** The names, dates, categories, ratings, review counts, imagery descriptions, and claimed locations above are transcribed from a user-provided narrative. They have **not** been independently checked against live Google Maps pages, archived records, original Street View imagery, or Google contribution histories. Treat them as leads for verification, not established facts.

## 2. Why this is interesting

The reported labels create an amusing contrast:

- “Homestay” suggests accommodation.
- “Rumah juang” evokes a campaign-era political label.
- “Historical landmark” gives the listing a heritage-site character.
- “Das Haus von” is German for roughly “the house of,” creating an unexpected multilingual flourish.

The comedy comes from the mismatch between a place on the ground and the labels attached to its map entry. It is not evidence that Google officially certified the place as a landmark, accommodation, or political site.

## 3. What the listing data can and cannot establish

A Google Maps POI listing may expose crowdsourced or platform-mediated metadata, including a name, category, rating, reviews, and location marker. Those fields do not, by themselves, establish:

1. who submitted or edited the listing;
2. whether a named person owns, occupies, or controls the property;
3. whether the category was selected by the property owner, a contributor, or a platform process;
4. whether reviewers visited the location;
5. whether ratings or review counts represent independent, genuine visits;
6. whether a Street View frame proves the listing's identity or historical significance.

A listing category is not a legal designation. A rating is not historical evidence. A map pin is not proof of ownership.

## 4. Timeline: a hypothesis, not a conclusion

If the reported dates and listing details are accurate, the sequence can be described as a change in *observed listing metadata* between 2022, 2024, and 2026.

It cannot yet support the stronger claim that the listing evolved in response to a person's political career. That would require dated captures of each listing, reliable location matching, and evidence of when and why each edit occurred.

**Safe wording:** “The three reported listings show different names and categories at different reported dates.”

**Unsupported wording:** “Google officially reclassified the residence as a historical landmark as its owner's career progressed.”

## 5. Verification checklist

Before upgrading this record from a user-supplied lead:

- [ ] Capture the exact Google Maps listing URL for each entry.
- [ ] Record retrieval date and time.
- [ ] Preserve screenshots showing the listing name, category, rating, and review count.
- [ ] Record the displayed coordinates and compare them cautiously.
- [ ] Distinguish listing data from Street View imagery dates.
- [ ] Check whether the listings are duplicates, moved pins, renamed entries, or unrelated places.
- [ ] Do not infer the contributor's identity or motive without evidence.
- [ ] Do not identify, expose, or investigate private residents.
- [ ] Keep original captures and a change log; never silently overwrite prior observations.

Suggested evidence statuses:

- `REPORTED`: stated by a source but not checked.
- `OBSERVED`: directly visible in a captured source.
- `CORROBORATED`: supported by independent evidence.
- `DISPUTED`: conflicting evidence exists.
- `UNKNOWN`: evidence is insufficient.
- `NOT_VERIFIED`: verification has not been completed.

Current status for all three entries: **REPORTED / NOT INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED**.

## 6. BLOON principle

> A map can show a pin. It cannot explain the whole story.

This case is useful as a small example of metadata drift, crowdsourced categorization, duplicate-POI risk, and the temptation to turn a funny coincidence into a causal narrative.
