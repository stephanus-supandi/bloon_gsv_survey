# Strellc i Epërm Street View Field Notes (April 2026)

**Project:** BLOON GSV Archaeology  
**Area:** Strellc i Epërm, Kosovo  
**Evidence basis:** User-described Street View frames and observations supplied in conversation. Images, coordinates, business listings, road-sign authority, and linguistic/administrative interpretations have not been independently verified for this note.

## Scope and method

This is a descriptive field note. It records visible details as reported and separates them from platform metadata and interpretation.

- **Visible evidence:** signs, lettering, buildings, objects, vehicles, landscape, and apparent placement in the frame.
- **Platform metadata:** map pins, business names/categories, ratings, opening status, and booking controls.
- **Interpretation:** possible explanations or relationships. These remain unverified unless supported by independent evidence.

No claim is made here about Kosovo's language policy, ethnic relations, political sentiment, business ownership, or the intent behind names.

## Observation set A — Village streetscape

### 1. Bilingual street-name plates on one pole

One described junction frame shows two street-name plates mounted on the same pole:

- Upper plate: RR. / UL. AVNI AVDIMETAJ
- Lower plate: RR. / UL. SHABAN RAMA

The supplied reading is that RR. abbreviates Albanian Rruga and UL. abbreviates Serbian Ulica. Both are written in Latin characters in the described frame.

This is recorded as a reported bilingual abbreviation on a street-name sign. The observation does not by itself establish the sign's issuing authority, legal status, or the broader language policy of the municipality or country.

The two visible street names are personal names. No biographical identification or explanation for the naming is inferred.

### 2. Blue house-number plate

A blue enamel-style plate marked 123 is described above a pair of weathered wooden doors on a structure the observer described as a barn or outbuilding. A smaller pedestrian door is visible to the right.

The record is limited to the visible number plate and its placement. The building's exact function is not established.

### 3. Braced utility pole with stickers/advertisements

A concrete utility pole is described as supported by two slanted braces leaning toward a conifer tree. Red-and-white stickers or advertisements with telephone numbers cover parts of the pole.

This is a further example in the survey of utility infrastructure also being used as an informal posting surface. The note does not identify the advertisers or infer whether the posting is authorized.

### 4. Advertisement mounted at the roof edge of a residence

A black-and-white banner showing a woman's face and the telephone number 049 909 533 is described at the roof edge of a single-storey residential building. An AUX-branded air-conditioning unit is visible on the brick wall below.

The observer could not confidently read the studio/business name and did not identify the advertised service. No salon, photography, or other business category is assigned.

### 5. Street name painted on the road

The road surface is described as carrying the text Rruga Enver Avdimetaj in large lettering.

This extends the survey's road-surface lettering observations beyond the previously described Pristina urban corridor. It is an observation from a village setting; it should not be generalized to all villages or all of Kosovo from this sample alone.

### 6. Human presence and environmental context

Across the five described frames, the observer reported no visible people and no motion-blurred human figures. Other visible/contextual details include clay roof tiles, exposed brick, overhead cables, conifers, willow trees, iron fencing on stone posts, a metal roadside drainage channel, and an overcast April sky.

The absence of visible people is limited to these five frames. It does not establish that the village was generally empty or inactive.

## Cross-batch notes

### A. Street-name repetition across media

The name Enver Avdimetaj is reported on asphalt, on a bilingual street-name pole, and on a business poster/window. This is a useful cross-medium observation. The media should remain distinct: road marking, street sign, and business display.

### B. Bilingual abbreviations are not the same issue as script choice

The reported RR./UL. plate uses Latin characters for both abbreviations while presenting two language forms. This is analytically distinct from the earlier Pristina observation of Cyrillic street lettering on asphalt. Do not collapse language, script, and medium into one category.

### C. Commercial use of the name Bill Clinton

The Pristina beauty-salon listing and the Strellc i Epërm café/bar name are separate reported observations. The name appears across two business categories and locations in this survey, but the sample does not establish why the name was chosen or whether the businesses are related.

### D. Replay accounting

Frames identified as duplicates should be marked as replayed and excluded from counts of new visual evidence. Their appearance may still matter for documenting the observer's navigation/history, but not as new evidence about the physical scene.
