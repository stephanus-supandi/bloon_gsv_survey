# Pristina Street View Notes (2025–2026)

**Project:** BLOON GSV Archaeology  
**Area:** Pristina, Kosovo  
**Evidence basis:** User-described Street View frames and observations supplied in conversation. The imagery, coordinates, Google Maps listings, business status, and road-code classifications have not been independently re-verified in this commit.

## Scope and handling rules

This note consolidates the Pristina observations discussed in this conversation. It is an observation log, not a claim about Kosovo's language policy, political history, business ownership, or the intent behind names and signs.

Keep separate:

1. **Visible frame evidence** — lettering, signs, buildings, vehicles, road markings, and their apparent physical placement.
2. **Platform metadata** — map pins, business categories, ratings, opening status, and booking controls.
3. **Interpretation** — possible relationships or explanations, which remain unverified unless supported independently.

Street View capture dates and the date of the user's viewing/session are not interchangeable. Repeated frames are not new visual evidence merely because they appear in a later batch.

## Cross-batch analytical notes

### A. Map labels and physical signs are not interchangeable

The “Passion Bill Clinton” example reinforces the need to keep platform metadata separate from visible signage. A map pin is a geospatial/platform assertion; a sign is a physical observation. Neither automatically validates the other.

This is consistent with the repository's general method: map/business labels are metadata, not ground truth.

### B. Business names can use personal names

The described names “Passion Bill Clinton” and “HILLARY” show personal/political names appearing in commercial naming in this corridor. The material does not establish why the names were chosen or whether the two storefronts are connected.

### C. Road-surface text has several distinct functions

The Pristina descriptions include personal street names in Latin and Cyrillic scripts, route identifiers, and traffic markings. These should remain separate categories rather than being collapsed into one generic “road text” class.

### D. A menu item can appear in a map pin name

A separate observation from another location in the same survey sequence reported the map-pin name “DENDENG & PUYUH BATOKOK LADO HIJAU ANAK BABE”. The phrase appears to name a specific dish/menu item rather than simply a generic shop type. This is a useful additional naming category, but it is not a Pristina observation and should not be merged into the Pristina dataset.
