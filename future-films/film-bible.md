# THE IMPOSSIBLE ARCHIVE — Film Bible

## Logline

When an independent researcher assembles years of ordinary Street View records from a residential area, small inconsistencies in the archive begin to resist a clean explanation. Every lead can be investigated; none can safely be turned into a grand conclusion without evidence.

## Format

- Short-film proof of concept: 6–10 minutes
- Expandable into an episodic investigative series
- Genre: archival mystery, documentary thriller, analog-horror atmosphere
- Language: Indonesian, with optional English subtitles
- Working banner: Future Films
- Source project: BLOON GSV Archaeology

## Creative north star

The film should feel frightening because the viewer cannot tell where observation ends and interpretation begins. Avoid cheap jump scares. Use silence, long holds, imperfect records, room tone, cursor movements, old timestamps, and the uncomfortable experience of checking the same detail again.

The story does not need a supernatural explanation. It does not need a conspiracy. Uncertainty itself is the engine.

## The three layers

### 1. The record
Show the original source as faithfully as possible: capture date when available, location context, source URL or archive reference, and what is actually visible. Preserve the distinction between capture date, upload date, and the date a viewer accessed the record.

### 2. The interpretation
Present competing explanations. State what each explanation accounts for, what it fails to account for, and what additional evidence would be required. Do not use ominous editing to make a weak hypothesis appear proven.

### 3. The film language
Sound design, color, pacing, fictional notes, and dramatized scenes may create mood. Any invented interface, invented document, or staged event must be treated as a film device, not an authentic record.

## Narrative structure

1. **Normal:** A familiar street and a simple research question.
2. **Comparison:** Two or more dated records are placed side by side.
3. **Mismatch:** A detail appears inconsistent, incomplete, or ambiguous.
4. **Investigation:** The researcher checks source provenance, location, dates, and alternative explanations.
5. **Dead end:** The evidence cannot support the dramatic conclusion the viewer wants.
6. **Residual question:** The film ends with a precise unanswered question, not a fabricated answer.

## Evidence protocol

Every real-world claim should have a source record with:
- unique evidence ID;
- source URL or file path;
- capture or publication date, if known;
- access date;
- exact location granularity justified by the source;
- neutral description of visible content;
- verification status;
- known limitations;
- hash for locally archived files where practical.

Never infer relationships from nearby house numbers alone. Never identify or accuse residents based on visual coincidence. Avoid publishing private personal details, vehicle plates, faces, or exact residential identifiers unless there is a clear lawful and ethical basis and the material is necessary.

## The uncertainty ledger

For each unresolved question, record:
- What is directly visible?
- What is inferred?
- What are at least two plausible alternative explanations?
- What evidence could distinguish those explanations?
- What remains unknown?
- What would make us abandon the current hypothesis?

An unanswered question is not a failure. It is a valid research result.

## Visual and sound direction

- Restrained black, off-white, faded amber, and muted blue-gray.
- Archival textures used sparingly; no fake scan lines over every shot.
- Slow zooms only when they guide attention to a real detail.
- Sound: fluorescent hum, distant traffic, room tone, keyboard clicks, a clock, and occasional silence.
- Avoid fake emergency broadcasts, fabricated official seals, or fake police/military documents that could be mistaken for genuine evidence.

## Ending principle

End on a question the available evidence genuinely cannot answer. Do not imply that a real person, family, neighborhood, or institution is hiding something merely because the archive is incomplete.

## Project status

CONCEPT ONLY. No case-specific anomaly, discovery, or real-world conclusion is asserted by this document. Any film case must be built from inspected and cited source material or clearly labeled fiction.
