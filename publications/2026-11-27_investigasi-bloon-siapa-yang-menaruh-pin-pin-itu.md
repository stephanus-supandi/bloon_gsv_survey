## Scope clarification

The intended framing is **global phenomenon, local investigation**:

- Global scope: the general ecosystem of digital map POIs, user contributions, business profiles, map moderation, and location-data quality across countries.
- Local scope: observations and screenshot-based checks are performed in specific places, beginning with the local survey area in Indonesia.
- Evidence rule: local observations do not establish a global prevalence rate or prove that the same mechanism is responsible everywhere.

The currently retrievable WordPress article subtitle refers to mismatches in a residential complex. This is the local observation context, not a claim that the phenomenon is confined to that complex. Future revisions should make the global/local distinction explicit in the subtitle and opening section.

## Main subjects covered

1. Who can contribute or change Google Maps place information: Maps users, business owners/managers, and Google's processing/moderation systems.
2. Possible explanations for visual mismatch: coordinate error, historical/stale information, remote contribution, repeated placement errors, and Street View perspective/display effects.
3. Separate evidence layers: physical objects, POI labels, coordinates, imagery dates, and contribution history where available.
4. Starlink: connectivity service only; no evidence links Starlink to the creation or placement of any observed pin.
5. Contributor identity: screenshots alone do not identify the person who created or edited a POI.
6. Verification workflow: preserve screenshot filenames, imagery dates, POI labels/categories, street context, physical appearance, coordinates when independently verified, and per-point status.
7. Evidence discipline: do not infer a common operator, coordinated activity, remote origin, or intentional manipulation from visual mismatch alone.

## Working hypotheses — not findings

- Incorrect POI coordinates.
- Out-of-date or historical business information.
- Remote edits by users who do not live in the mapped area.
- Repeated errors from a shared source or workflow.
- Apparent misalignment caused by panorama perspective or interface rendering.

None of these hypotheses has been established as the cause for all observed pins.
