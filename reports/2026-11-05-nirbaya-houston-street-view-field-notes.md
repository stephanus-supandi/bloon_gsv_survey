## 7. Corrections and scope discipline

1. **Do not convert map-pin mismatch into business history.** A pin appearing over several scenes is a spatial/data-quality problem until the physical premises and business identity are independently resolved.
2. **Do not convert pin persistence into a closure narrative.** Jack’s Grocery’s reported 2018/2026 scene contrast does not prove bankruptcy, permanent closure, relocation, or database error.
3. **Do not infer legal status from a suffix alone.** “P.C.” is a naming/legal-form clue, not a substitute for a corporate registry check.
4. **Do not infer authorized brand relationships from a brand name.** “Pirelli” plus an auto-repair category is not proof of a franchise or authorized workshop.
5. **Do not infer demographics from advertising language.** A Spanish-language billboard is an advertising observation, not a demographic survey.
6. **Do not infer tenant status from a directory alone.** A monument sign records displayed names; it does not establish active leases or current operations.
7. **Do not treat map categories as ground truth.** Categories such as “Brunch restaurant” and “Auto repair shop” are platform metadata.
8. **Keep lexical uncertainty explicit.** “GBAP,” “Radema,” and “Jegger” remain unresolved strings unless independently sourced.

## 9. Repository method reminder

The survey continues to distinguish:

- **OBSERVED / REPORTED:** wording or visual condition described in a supplied frame note.
- **MATCH:** a spatial or entity link supported by sufficient identifiers.
- **CHANGE:** a cross-date difference supported by comparable imagery and location identity.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** an interpretation requiring independent tests.
- **UNKNOWN:** unresolved identity, meaning, cause, or relationship.

**Core rule:** Street View records a visual scene at a particular capture time. Map pins and categories are platform metadata. Neither, alone, is a business registry, legal-property record, demographic survey, environmental audit, or economic appraisal.
