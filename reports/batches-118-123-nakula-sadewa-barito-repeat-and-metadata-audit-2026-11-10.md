## Cross-batch controls and cautions

### 1. Repeat appearance is not independent evidence

A repeated frame, listing, sign, or place-name in adjacent batches must not be counted as a new observation until object identity, image identity, and capture provenance are distinguished. Batch 118 is explicitly treated as a repeat of Batch 117, not as evidence of stagnation.

### 2. Capture date is not listing-update date

A Street View capture label such as Mar 2025 dates the imagery, not every Google Maps field shown alongside it. Business name, category, pin position, address, rating, and current status may have different update histories. Unless independently available, record listing-update date as UNKNOWN.

Recommended fields:
- STREET_VIEW_CAPTURE_DATE
- LISTING_OBSERVED_AT
- LISTING_UPDATE_DATE = UNKNOWN unless sourced
- PHYSICAL_SIGN_TEXT
- PIN_CATEGORY
- RELATION_STATUS
- BUSINESS_OPERATION_STATUS = UNKNOWN unless directly verified

### 3. Suggested relation statuses

Use separate statuses rather than a single generic “mismatch” label:
- MATCH_OBSERVED — physical sign and listing appear compatible in the observed frame.
- APPARENT_MISMATCH — visible pin/category and physical sign appear inconsistent; cause unresolved.
- AMBIGUOUS_RELATION — multiple pins/signs/facades appear related, but identity or ownership is unknown.
- INSUFFICIENT_VISUAL_EVIDENCE — the image does not support a reliable match.
- REPEAT_OBSERVATION — content appears repeated; do not count as a new independent POI without identity verification.
- CLAIM_OBSERVED_NOT_VERIFIED — a sign/banner makes a claim, but operation or truth of the claim is unverified.

### 4. Non-commercial elements are retained separately

The RT/RW security post, FBR-branded secretariat sign, and possibly FBR-associated flag on a gate are non-commodity observations. They must not be forced into the commodity table or expanded into claims about membership, control, political influence, ethnicity, or conflict.

### 5. Co-location is not a cluster

Two or more signs or services in one frame or capture breath establish co-presence only. They do not establish a cluster, shared ownership, operational coordination, or a common social function.
