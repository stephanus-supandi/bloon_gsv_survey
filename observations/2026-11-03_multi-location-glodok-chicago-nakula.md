## 1. Evidence rules

- **REPORTED VISIBLE:** A feature the observer says is visible in a frame.
- **MAP METADATA:** A name, category, review count, opening-hours panel, pin, or Plus Code shown by a mapping interface. Metadata is not ground truth.
- **TEMPORAL COMPARISON:** Capture labels/dates are recorded as supplied; they do not alone prove a physical or business change.
- **UNKNOWN / UNVERIFIED:** Identity, purpose, ownership, operational status, exact coordinates, or causal relationship not established by the supplied description.

No Street View imagery is redistributed in this record. A private-location code and a phone number from a rental sign are omitted from this public note.

## 7. Cross-batch descriptive categories

The descriptions add examples to the working observation vocabulary:

1. **Infrastructure text media:** safety warnings, complaint-channel QR instructions, project identifiers, station names, route diagrams, and package codes on construction barriers.
2. **Public wayfinding:** directional signs naming destinations, distinct from warning or prohibition signage.
3. **Map metadata vs physical frontage:** pins on closed shutters or alleys without an obvious business frontage; these are mismatch candidates, not proof of incorrect geocoding.
4. **Multiple pins / access points:** multiple map entries associated with different entrances of one named institution; official mapping is needed to verify the relationship.
5. **Naming patterns:** business names containing numbers, mythological references, unexplained initials, or secondary titles.
6. **Temporal text variation:** “Nakula 3” vs “Nakula 3D” across supplied capture labels; no road-name change is asserted.
7. **Contributor metadata:** a named contributor/date header on a described 2018 image; platform attribution semantics are not established.
8. **Co-location:** BTS infrastructure and a mosque dome in one visual corridor; proximity does not prove shared ownership or function.

These are descriptive classifications, not claims that the categories are unique, exhaustive, or newly discovered in the world.

## 8. Limitations and verification requirements

- Original frames, frame URLs, coordinates, panorama IDs, and full capture metadata were not included in the text supplied for this update.
- Capture dates, times, battery percentages, place identifications, and visible-text transcriptions are observer-supplied.
- A map pin or business panel is metadata, not ground truth.
- Visual proximity does not establish ownership, affiliation, operational relationship, or infrastructure connectivity.
- Repeated appearance is not automatically a new discovery; it may be a re-observation or continuity record.
- Different labels across years do not by themselves establish a physical change.
- No person is identified from appearance. Professional names are included only where described as public signage / map labels.
- Exact private-location code and phone number are omitted from this public record.
- No independent verification of business status, medical credentials, project-package scope, or institutional entrance mapping has been performed.
